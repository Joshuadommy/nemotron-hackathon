## §1. Definitions
- "Automated Employment Decision Tool" (AEDT): any computational process, derived from machine learning, statistical modeling, data analytics, or artificial intelligence, that issues a simplified output (score, classification, recommendation) used to substantially assist or replace discretionary decision-making for employment decisions.
- "Substantially assist or replace": (a) relying solely on a simplified output without other factors; (b) using a simplified output as one of a set of criteria where the output is weighted more than any other criterion; or (c) using a simplified output to overrule conclusions derived from other factors including human decision-making.
- "Employment decision": to screen candidates for employment or employees for promotion within the City.

## §2. Bias Audit Requirement
An employer or employment agency shall not use an AEDT to screen a candidate or employee unless:
(a) The tool has been the subject of a bias audit conducted no more than one year prior to use.
(b) A summary of the most recent bias audit and the distribution date of the tool to which it applies has been made publicly available on the employer or employment agency's website prior to use.

A "bias audit" must be conducted by an independent auditor and calculate, at minimum:
- The selection rate for each race/ethnicity and sex category.
- The impact ratio for each race/ethnicity and sex category.
- Where the AEDT scores candidates, the scoring rate and impact ratio of scores for each category.
- Intersectional categories of sex × race/ethnicity.

## §3. Candidate Notification Requirement
At least ten business days before using an AEDT, the employer must:
(a) Notify each candidate or employee who resides in NYC that an AEDT will be used in connection with the assessment.
(b) Allow the candidate to request an alternative selection process or accommodation.
(c) Disclose the job qualifications and characteristics the AEDT will use in the assessment.
(d) Disclose information about the type of data collected, the source of the data, and the employer's data retention policy (or provide instructions on how to make a written request for this information; response required within 30 days).

## §4. Publication Requirement
The summary of the bias audit results, including the source and explanation of the data used, the number of individuals the AEDT assessed who fall within an unknown category, and the date the AEDT was first used, must be publicly accessible on the careers or jobs section of the employer's website for at least six months after the latest use of the AEDT.

## §5. Exclusions
- AEDTs that do not automate, support, substantially assist, or replace discretionary decisions by employers.
- Junk-email filters, firewalls, antivirus software, calculators, spreadsheets, databases.

## §6. Common Violations to Watch For
- Using a vendor-built AEDT that has not been independently audited within the last 12 months.
- Failing to publish the bias audit summary on a public-facing page.
- Notifying candidates after the AEDT has already been used.
- Treating a national tool roll-out as exempt simply because the employer is not headquartered in NYC (the law applies to the position location or candidate residence).
