## §5 Required Disclosures
Before asking applicants to submit video interviews analyzed by AI, the employer must:
(a) Notify each applicant in writing before the interview that artificial intelligence may be used to analyze the applicant's video interview and consider the applicant's fitness for the position.
(b) Provide each applicant with information before the interview explaining how the AI works and what general types of characteristics it uses to evaluate applicants.
(c) Obtain, before the interview, consent from the applicant to be evaluated by the AI program as described in the information provided.

An employer may not use AI to evaluate applicants who have not consented to the use of AI analysis.

## §10 Sharing Restrictions
Employers may share applicant videos only with persons whose expertise or technology is necessary to evaluate an applicant's fitness for a position.

## §15 Destruction of Videos
Upon request from the applicant, an employer must — within 30 days of receipt of the request — destroy all copies of the applicant's video interviews, including copies shared with other persons. Persons who received copies must also destroy them within 30 days.

## §20 Demographic Reporting (added 2022)
Employers that rely solely upon an AI analysis of a video interview to determine whether an applicant will be selected for an in-person interview must collect and report annually to the Illinois Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity (DCEO):
(a) The race and ethnicity of applicants who are and are not afforded the in-person interview after the use of artificial intelligence analysis.
(b) The race and ethnicity of applicants who are hired.

The data is used by the Department to analyze whether such use results in racial bias.

## §Common Violations to Watch For
- Asking applicants to record a video interview analyzed by AI without prior written notification or consent.
- Failing to destroy applicant video within 30 days of a written destruction request.
- Sharing video with vendors whose involvement is not "necessary" to evaluate fitness.
- Skipping annual demographic reporting when AI is the sole gate to in-person interviews.
