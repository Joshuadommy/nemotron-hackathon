## §Art.5 Prohibited AI Practices
The following are prohibited:
(a) Subliminal, manipulative, or deceptive techniques distorting behavior causing significant harm.
(b) Exploiting vulnerabilities (age, disability, socio-economic) to materially distort behavior.
(c) Social scoring by public authorities or on their behalf leading to detrimental treatment.
(d) Predictive policing based solely on profiling or personality traits.
(e) Untargeted scraping of facial images from internet/CCTV for facial recognition databases.
(f) Emotion recognition in workplaces and educational institutions (with narrow safety/medical exception).
(g) Biometric categorization to infer race, political opinion, trade union membership, religion, sex life, or sexual orientation.
(h) Real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces for law enforcement (with narrow exceptions).

Hiring application: Emotion recognition during interviews is explicitly prohibited in the workplace context, including pre-employment interviews where the candidate is a prospective worker.

## §Art.6 / Annex III High-Risk AI Systems
AI systems are "high-risk" when used in the areas listed in Annex III. Relevant categories include:

Annex III(4) — Employment, workers management, and access to self-employment:
(a) AI systems intended to be used for the recruitment or selection of natural persons, in particular to place targeted job advertisements, to analyze and filter job applications, and to evaluate candidates.
(b) AI systems intended to be used to make decisions affecting terms of work-related relationships, the promotion or termination of work-related contractual relationships, to allocate tasks based on individual behavior or personal traits, or to monitor and evaluate the performance and behavior of persons.

A hiring AI system is high-risk by default; an exception under Art.6(3) applies only when the system performs a narrow procedural task, improves the result of a previously completed human activity, detects decision-making patterns without replacing or influencing human assessment, or is preparatory to an assessment relevant to Annex III purposes.

## §Art.8–17 Provider Obligations for High-Risk Systems
Providers of high-risk AI systems must:
- Art.9 — Establish, document, and maintain a risk management system across the lifecycle.
- Art.10 — Ensure training, validation, and test datasets are relevant, representative, free of errors, and complete to the best extent possible; examine for biases likely to affect health, safety, or fundamental rights.
- Art.11 — Draw up technical documentation before placing on the market.
- Art.12 — Automatic logging of events during operation.
- Art.13 — Sufficient transparency to enable deployers to interpret outputs and use them appropriately; instructions for use.
- Art.14 — Effective human oversight design.
- Art.15 — Appropriate level of accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity throughout the lifecycle.
- Art.16 — Quality management system.
- Art.17 — Conformity assessment and CE marking before placing on the market.

## §Art.26 Deployer Obligations
Deployers (the employer using the tool) must:
- Use the system in accordance with the instructions for use.
- Assign human oversight to natural persons with the necessary competence, training, authority, and support.
- Ensure input data is relevant and sufficiently representative for the intended purpose (to the extent the deployer exercises control over it).
- Monitor the operation of the system and inform the provider of serious incidents.
- Keep logs automatically generated by the system for an appropriate period (at least six months unless specified otherwise).
- Inform workers' representatives and affected workers before putting a high-risk AI system into service in the workplace.

## §Art.27 Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA)
Before deploying a high-risk AI system listed in Annex III (with limited exceptions for certain financial-services and credit-scoring use cases), deployers that are bodies governed by public law, private operators providing public services, or deployers of credit-scoring or life-insurance systems must perform an FRIA covering: process, period and frequency of use, categories of persons affected, specific risks of harm, human oversight measures, and remediation if risks materialize.

## §Art.50 Transparency Obligations
- AI systems interacting with natural persons must inform the person they are interacting with an AI (unless obvious).
- Providers/deployers of emotion-recognition or biometric-categorization systems (where lawful) must inform exposed persons.
- Deep-fake content must be labeled as artificially generated/manipulated.

## §Common Violations to Watch For
- Treating a hiring AI as low-risk because "a human reviews the output" without meeting Art.6(3) exception criteria.
- Deploying emotion-recognition video interviews in the EU — outright prohibited.
- Failing to inform candidates/employees and workers' representatives.
- Skipping the FRIA for public-sector or public-service deployments.
- Provider failure to perform conformity assessment / CE marking before EU market placement.
