## §6-1-1701 Definitions (selected)
- "Algorithmic discrimination": any condition in which the use of an AI system results in unlawful differential treatment or impact that disfavors an individual or group on the basis of actual or perceived age, color, disability, ethnicity, genetic information, limited English proficiency, national origin, race, religion, reproductive health, sex, veteran status, or other class protected under state or federal law.
- "Consequential decision": a decision that has a material legal or similarly significant effect on the provision or denial to any consumer of (a) education enrollment or opportunity; (b) employment or an employment opportunity; (c) a financial or lending service; (d) an essential government service; (e) health care services; (f) housing; (g) insurance; or (h) a legal service.
- "Developer": a person doing business in Colorado that develops or intentionally and substantially modifies a high-risk AI system.
- "Deployer": a person doing business in Colorado that uses a high-risk AI system.
- "Substantial factor": a factor that (i) assists in making a consequential decision; (ii) is capable of altering the outcome of a consequential decision; and (iii) is generated by an AI system.

## §6-1-1702 Developer Duties
A developer must use reasonable care to protect consumers from any known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination. Developers must make available to deployers:
- A general statement describing reasonably foreseeable uses and known harmful or inappropriate uses.
- Documentation describing: high-level data summary used to train, known/foreseeable limitations of the system including algorithmic-discrimination risks, purpose, intended benefits, uses, and how the system was evaluated for performance and mitigation of discrimination.
- Information necessary for a deployer to complete an impact assessment.
- A public statement summarizing the types of high-risk systems the developer has developed or substantially modified and how the developer manages known/foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination.

Developers must disclose known/foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination to the Colorado AG and to known deployers within 90 days of discovery.

## §6-1-1703 Deployer Duties
A deployer must use reasonable care to protect consumers from algorithmic discrimination. Specifically:
- Implement a risk management policy and program governing the deployer's use of high-risk AI systems.
- Complete an impact assessment for each high-risk AI system before deployment and at least annually thereafter, and within 90 days after any intentional and substantial modification.
- Notify a consumer that a high-risk AI system has been deployed to make, or be a substantial factor in making, a consequential decision concerning them — before or at the time of the decision.
- When a consequential decision is adverse, provide the consumer with: (i) the principal reason(s) for the decision, including the degree to which the AI system contributed and the type of data processed and its source; (ii) an opportunity to correct inaccurate personal data; and (iii) an opportunity to appeal — including, if technically feasible, human review.
- Maintain a publicly available statement summarizing the types of high-risk AI systems currently deployed and how the deployer manages risks of algorithmic discrimination.

## §6-1-1704 Impact Assessment Contents
At minimum: statement of purpose and intended use; analysis of risks of algorithmic discrimination and mitigation steps; description of categories of data the system processes as inputs and the outputs it produces; metrics used to evaluate performance and limitations; description of transparency measures; description of post-deployment monitoring and user safeguards; for modifications, the extent of modification and the reasons.

## §6-1-1706 Affirmative Defense / Rebuttable Presumption
A deployer or developer who complies with a recognized risk-management framework (e.g., NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001) is presumed to have used reasonable care.

## §Common Violations to Watch For
- Deploying a high-risk AI system without an impact assessment.
- Failing to notify a Colorado consumer that AI was a substantial factor in an adverse hiring decision.
- Not providing an appeal mechanism (with human review where technically feasible) for adverse consequential decisions.
- Developer failure to disclose known algorithmic-discrimination risks to deployers and to the AG.
