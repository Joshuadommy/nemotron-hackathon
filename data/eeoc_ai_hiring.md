## §1. Scope
Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 prohibits employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation, gender identity), or national origin. This applies regardless of whether decisions are made by humans, software, or a combination.

The "Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures" (UGESP, 29 CFR Part 1607) apply to any "selection procedure" used as a basis for an employment decision — including algorithmic tools, resume screeners, chatbots, video-interview analyzers, and personality tests scored by software.

## §2. The Four-Fifths Rule (Disparate Impact)
A selection procedure is generally regarded as having an adverse impact on a protected group if its selection rate for that group is less than 80% (four-fifths) of the selection rate of the group with the highest selection rate.

Example: If the selection rate for white applicants is 60% and the selection rate for Black applicants is 45%, the impact ratio is 45/60 = 0.75 < 0.80 → potential adverse impact, requiring validation.

The four-fifths rule is a rule of thumb; statistical significance may apply with large applicant pools.

## §3. Employer Responsibility for Vendor Tools
An employer may be responsible under Title VII for the use of an algorithmic decision-making tool even if the tool was designed or administered by a vendor. Common arguments that DO NOT relieve liability:
- "The vendor said it was bias-tested." Employer must independently confirm.
- "We didn't know how the model worked." Employer must conduct or obtain disparate-impact testing.
- "It's just one factor among many." If the tool meaningfully influences outcomes, it is a selection procedure.

## §4. Validation Requirement
If a selection procedure produces adverse impact, the employer must show the procedure is "job related for the position in question and consistent with business necessity." Acceptable validation methods under UGESP:
- Criterion-related validity (statistical correlation with job performance).
- Content validity (representative sample of job tasks/knowledge).
- Construct validity (measures an identifiable trait important for the job).

Even with validation, an employer must consider whether an "alternative selection procedure" with less adverse impact would serve the same business purpose.

## §5. Reasonable Accommodation (ADA / Title VII religious)
Algorithmic tools that screen out applicants with disabilities may violate the ADA. Employers must:
- Offer alternative formats when an AEDT cannot be used by an applicant with a disability.
- Avoid tools that measure traits unrelated to job-essential functions if they disadvantage protected groups.

## §6. Recordkeeping
Employers must keep records of applications and selection outcomes by race, sex, and ethnicity for at least one year (longer for federal contractors) to enable adverse-impact analysis.

## §7. Common Violations to Watch For
- Deploying an AI hiring tool without measuring selection rates by demographic group.
- Relying on a vendor's bias claim without an independent audit.
- Using a video-interview model that screens out candidates with speech or motor disabilities.
- Treating the four-fifths rule as a safe harbor (it is a starting screen, not a defense).
