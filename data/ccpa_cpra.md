## §1798.100 Right to Know
A consumer (including a CA applicant or employee) has the right to request that a business disclose the categories and specific pieces of personal information the business has collected about them, the categories of sources, business or commercial purpose, and categories of third parties to whom the information is disclosed.

## §1798.105 Right to Delete
A consumer has the right to request deletion of personal information collected from them, subject to enumerated exceptions (legal obligations, security, internal uses reasonably aligned with consumer expectations, etc.).

## §1798.106 Right to Correct
A consumer has the right to request correction of inaccurate personal information.

## §1798.110 / §1798.115 Disclosure Categories
The business must disclose categories of personal information collected, sources, purposes, and third-party recipients.

## §1798.120 Right to Opt Out of Sale/Sharing
A consumer has the right to opt out of the sale or sharing of their personal information. The business must provide a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link and recognize the Global Privacy Control (GPC) signal.

## §1798.121 Right to Limit Use of Sensitive Personal Information
"Sensitive Personal Information" (SPI) includes SSN, driver's license, financial account, precise geolocation, race/ethnicity, religious beliefs, union membership, contents of mail/email/text not addressed to the business, genetic data, biometric data for unique identification, health information, and information about sex life or sexual orientation. Consumers have the right to limit use of SPI to that necessary to perform the service.

## §1798.130 Notice at Collection
At or before the point of collection, businesses must inform consumers of: categories of personal information to be collected, purposes, whether information is sold/shared, length of retention. For employees and applicants, a separate or integrated employee/applicant notice is required.

## §1798.185(a)(16) — Automated Decisionmaking Technology (ADMT) Regulations
The CPPA is authorized to issue regulations governing access and opt-out rights with respect to businesses' use of "automated decisionmaking technology, including profiling," and require businesses' response to access requests to include "meaningful information about the logic involved in such decisionmaking processes, as well as a description of the likely outcome of the process with respect to the consumer."

CPPA ADMT regulations (finalized 2024–2025) require:
- Pre-use notice to consumers about ADMT use for significant decisions (employment, education, housing, financial, healthcare, essential goods/services).
- Right to opt out of ADMT for significant decisions (with limited exceptions including security and where opting out is incompatible with the legitimate use, e.g., admissions/hiring narrow exception requires human appeal).
- Right to access information about how ADMT was used in a decision about the consumer.
- Risk assessment requirement before initiating ADMT processing that presents significant risks to consumer privacy.

## §1798.155 Enforcement
The CPPA may issue administrative fines without right to cure (the 30-day cure period was removed by CPRA).

## §Common Violations to Watch For
- Treating CA applicants/employees as outside CCPA scope (the employee exemption sunset on 2023-01-01).
- Using an AI hiring tool on CA candidates without a pre-use ADMT notice.
- Failing to provide a meaningful human-review/appeal option for ADMT decisions.
- Skipping the risk assessment required before deploying ADMT for significant decisions.
- Collecting race/ethnicity self-identification data without recognizing it as SPI.
